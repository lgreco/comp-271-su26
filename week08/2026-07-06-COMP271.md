## Revisiting Reversal of a Doubly Linked List
We opened by revisiting the linked list reversal assignment, since a few of us found the logic tricky. With Temeeka's questions guiding the discussion, we worked through the idea on a virtual whiteboard: a doubly linked list has a next pointer and a previous pointer per node, and reversing it means re-wiring both directions, not just swapping the head and tail labels. We traced a cursor across the list, activating each node's previous pointer to point backward, then made a second pass to clear the old next pointers — or, more efficiently, used a temporary pointer to do both in a single pass. We emphasized that working these problems out on paper or a whiteboard before coding — visualizing nodes as people pointing with their arms, for example — builds the kind of thinking that matters most in a technical interview or on a team.

## Removing the Tail and the Head
We then looked at two special-case removals from a fully connected doubly linked list. To remove the tail, we captured the tail's previous node as the new tail, set its next pointer to none, and moved the tail label there — three constant-time steps, regardless of list size. With Temeeka's help working through the logic, we did the symmetric operation for removing the head: capture the head's next node as the candidate, set its previous pointer to none, and move the head label. Both operations run in $\mathcal O(1)$ time.

## Toward Queues and Stacks
We closed by noting that since we can add and remove nodes from either end of the list in constant time, we can build two classic abstractions on top of it: a queue (first in, first out) and a stack (last in, first out) — the topic for tomorrow.

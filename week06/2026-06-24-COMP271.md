## Recap: Train Line and Tail Pointer

We opened by reviewing where we left off: the train line data structure with both a `head` and a `tail` pointer. Adding a station at the end is now $O(1)$ — we go straight to the tail rather than traversing the whole line. We also touched on asymptotic complexity at a practical level: polynomial time ($n^2$, $n^3$) stays manageable as problems grow, while exponential time ($2^n$, $3^n$) quickly becomes infeasible. For $n = 100$, $n^2 = 10{,}000$ while $2^{100} \approx 10^{30}$ — longer than the age of the universe in seconds. Nested loops multiply complexity: each additional nesting level raises the exponent by one.

## Implementing `contains`

We wrote a `contains` method that searches the train line for a named station and returns a boolean. Because our object is not a list, tuple, or string, a `for` loop raises "object is not iterable." Instead, we used a **cursor** starting at `self.head` and a `while` loop with two exit conditions: stop when the cursor reaches `None` (end of line) or when `result` becomes `True` (station found). We hit an infinite loop mid-demo — with Temeeka and Alexander's help, we spotted that `cursor.getNext()` was being called but never assigned back to `cursor`.

## Making the Object Iterable with `__iter__`

Once we fixed the bug, we added a special `__iter__` method using the same cursor pattern and the `yield` keyword. Unlike `return`, which exits the method immediately, `yield` passes the current value out and *resumes* on the next iteration. With `__iter__` defined, `for x in red_line_southbound:` worked as expected.

## Style: `while` over `break`

We discussed a stylistic preference: rather than using a `for` loop with a `break` statement, a `while` loop expresses the search intent directly — "keep going until found or exhausted." The assignment for Monday is to implement the remaining contract methods for `TrainLine`.
